# Story Skeletons

**One line:** Reusable narrative spines — a small set of shapes a story can take — that you hang specific material on.

**Invented by:** Christopher Booker (*The Seven Basic Plots*, 2004) for the taxonomy; Kurt Vonnegut's "shapes of stories" lecture for the emotional-arc view; Joseph Campbell / the monomyth for the quest spine. (Use as a generative tool, not a law — see caveat.)

## When to use

- TEXT domain, narrative: fiction, a script, a pitch, a brand story, even structuring a talk.
- You have material/characters but no *shape* to organize them.

## The skeletons

| Shape | Spine | The turn |
|---|---|---|
| **Overcoming the Monster** | A threat looms; hero confronts and defeats it. | The moment the monster seems to win. |
| **Rags to Riches** | Nobody rises to somebody — then risks losing it. | The false summit, then the real growth. |
| **The Quest** | A goal far away; a journey with companions and trials. | The temptation to abandon the goal. |
| **Voyage and Return** | Into a strange world, changed, back home. | Realizing the strange world's rule. |
| **Comedy** | Confusion/misunderstanding resolves into union. | The tangle at its most knotted. |
| **Tragedy** | A flaw drives an inevitable fall. | The point where it could still have been avoided. |
| **Rebirth** | A dark force grips the hero until redemption. | The thaw. |

Vonnegut's arcs (emotional, plot on a good-fortune axis over time) are a complementary lens: *Man in Hole* (down then up), *Boy Meets Girl* (up, down, up), *Cinderella* (steps up, crash, soar), *Kafka* (starts bad, gets worse).

## The method

1. **Pick the shape** your material already *wants* — don't force a quest onto a tragedy.
2. **Map your specifics onto the spine.** Who's the monster? What's the strange world? What's the flaw?
3. **Locate the turn.** Every skeleton has one hinge moment; find yours — it's where the story lives.
4. **Then break the skeleton deliberately** if you want freshness — a *Voyage and Return* where the hero refuses to go home; a *Rags to Riches* that's really a tragedy.

## Caveat

These are generative scaffolds, not a theory of all stories. Their value is traction when you're blank, and a known shape to subvert. Don't defend them as universal; use them and move on.

## Worked example

Material: a burned-out dev quits to fix one tiny open-source tool.
- Shape chosen: **Voyage and Return** (into the strange world of unpaid maintenance, back changed).
- Turn: discovering the "small tool" quietly runs critical infrastructure — the strange world's rule (invisible load-bearing labor).
- That turn becomes the story's point and its title.

## Output shape

Name the shape, map the specifics, mark the turn. Offer one "break the skeleton" variant for freshness.
