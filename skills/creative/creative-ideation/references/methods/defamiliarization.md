# Defamiliarization

**One line:** Make the familiar strange — describe a known thing as if seen for the first time, stripping the automatic words we use to skip past it.

**Invented by:** Viktor Shklovsky (*ostranenie*, "Art as Technique", 1917, Russian Formalism). Kin: Brecht's *Verfremdungseffekt* in theatre.

## When to use

- Phase = REFINING, TEXT domain: prose feels flat, clichéd, or invisible; the reader's eye slides off.
- You want a fresh angle on an over-written subject (love, death, the city, grief).

## The idea

Habit makes perception automatic — we stop *seeing* the things we know. Shklovsky: art exists "to make the stone stony." The technique slows perception down by refusing the ready-made label, forcing the reader (and you) to actually encounter the thing. Tolstoy describing an opera as meaningless painted cardboard and shouting people; a horse narrating human property as an absurd custom — both make a known thing newly visible.

## The method

1. **Pick the over-familiar thing** (object, routine, emotion, institution).
2. **Ban its usual words.** Can't say "phone", "love", "meeting", "money" — describe only what is physically happening.
3. **Narrate from an estranged position** — a visitor, a child, an animal, a Martian, someone from another century, a literal-minded machine.
4. **Stay concrete and sensory.** No explaining; only describing what is seen/heard/felt.
5. **Stop before you name it.** Let the reader do the recognition.

## Worked example

Subject: checking your phone (clichéd to write about).
- Banned words: phone, screen, scroll, app, notification.
- Estranged narration: *"She lifts the warm black mirror to her face at a measured distance, the way one checks whether a coal is still lit. Light pours up onto her chin. Her thumb strokes the glass in short downward motions, over and over, grooming an animal that never looks back, and her eyes do the small rapid work of someone reading a horizon for weather."*

The familiar act becomes visible again — and the simile (grooming an animal that never looks back) is the idea the refined passage was missing.

## Output shape

Produce the estranged passage itself, then (optionally) one line naming the fresh image/angle it surfaced that the user can carry back into the real draft.
