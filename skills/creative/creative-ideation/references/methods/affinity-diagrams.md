# Affinity Diagrams

**One line:** Cluster raw observations bottom-up — without pre-set categories — until themes emerge from the data itself.

**Invented by:** Jiro Kawakita (the "KJ Method", 1960s Japan); a staple of design research and the Japanese quality movement.

## When to use

- Phase = SYNTHESIZING: the user has a pile of notes, interview quotes, survey answers, support tickets, observations — and needs the *insight*, not more data.
- You're tempted to impose obvious top-down buckets; resist, and let structure surface.

## The core discipline: bottom-up, not top-down

The whole value is that you **do not decide the categories in advance**. Pre-made buckets ("pricing / UX / bugs") just confirm what you already believed. Affinity diagramming forces the themes to come *from* the observations, which is where the surprising insight lives.

## The method

1. **One observation per card.** Break the raw material into atomic, concrete notes — a single quote, a single observed behavior. Keep the subject's own words.
2. **Spread them all out.** See everything at once.
3. **Group by felt affinity, silently.** Put cards that "belong together" near each other *without naming the group yet*. If a card fits two groups, duplicate it. Move things around freely.
4. **Name the clusters last.** Once a group is stable, write the theme — ideally as a *sentence that makes a claim*, not a one-word label. "Users abandon at payment" → better: "Users trust us until we ask for a card, then reconsider the whole thing."
5. **Find relationships between clusters.** Arrows: causes, tensions, sequences. The map between themes is often the real finding.
6. **Write the one insight.** The single sentence you didn't know before you started.

## Worked example

40 support tickets. Resist sorting by product-area. Clustering by felt affinity, a group forms around messages that all share *"I thought it was broken but it was working"*. Named as a claim: **"Our slowest operations give no feedback, so users assume failure and retry, creating the load that makes them slower."** That causal loop — invisible in a top-down "performance" bucket — is the insight and the fix.

## Output shape

The named clusters (as claim-sentences), the key relationships between them, and the one-sentence insight. Show a couple of representative raw observations under each cluster so the user trusts the grounding.
