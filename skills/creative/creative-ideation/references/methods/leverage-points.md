# Leverage Points

**One line:** In any system, some places to intervene are far more powerful than others; find the high-leverage ones instead of pushing where it's obvious.

**Invented by:** Donella Meadows ("Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System", 1999; *Thinking in Systems*, 2008).

## When to use

- SYSTEM domain: an organization, a community, a market, a policy, an ecology — anywhere feedback loops and structure matter more than any single part.
- The "obvious" fix (more money, more rules, try harder) isn't working.

## The idea

People habitually push on the *least* powerful leverage points — usually numbers (budgets, targets, subsidies) — because they're visible and easy. Meadows ranks the places to intervene from weakest to strongest. Higher leverage is harder to change but moves the whole system. Counterintuitively, pushing a weak lever harder often does nothing; a small change at a strong point can transform everything.

## The ladder (weak → strong)

1. **Constants, parameters, numbers** (budgets, tax rates). Weak — you can fiddle forever.
2. **Buffer sizes** (stocks relative to flows).
3. **Stock-and-flow structures** (physical plumbing).
4. **Delays** in feedback loops.
5. **Balancing (negative) feedback loops** — their strength relative to the impact they correct.
6. **Reinforcing (positive) feedback loops** — slow the runaway ones.
7. **Information flows** — who has access to what. (Often cheap and powerful: make the hidden visible.)
8. **Rules** — incentives, punishments, constraints.
9. **Self-organization** — the power to add/change/evolve system structure.
10. **Goals** — the purpose the whole system is tuned toward.
11. **Paradigms** — the shared mindset the goals arise from.
12. **The power to transcend paradigms.**

## The method

1. **Draw the system** — stocks, flows, the key feedback loops, who sees what.
2. **Locate where interventions are currently aimed** (usually #1–2, the weak end).
3. **Climb the ladder:** for the same problem, what would changing an *information flow* (#7), a *rule* (#8), the *goal* (#10), or the *paradigm* (#11) do?
4. **Pick the highest leverage you can actually reach,** and name the lower-leverage support actions around it.

## Worked example

Problem: a town's recycling rate is stuck. Current intervention: #1, a bigger ad budget (weak).
- #7 Information: give each household a monthly comparison to its neighbors' recycling (making the invisible visible) — cheap, high-leverage, well-evidenced.
- #8 Rules: charge by weight of landfill trash, not a flat fee — change the incentive.
- #10 Goal: shift the system's goal from "recycle more" to "produce less waste", which reframes everything upstream.
Recommendation: deploy #7 now (reachable, strong), lobby for #8, and name #10 as the real target.

## Output shape

The system sketch, where effort is currently (mis)aimed, the higher-leverage options by rung, and a concrete recommendation that's both high-leverage and reachable.
