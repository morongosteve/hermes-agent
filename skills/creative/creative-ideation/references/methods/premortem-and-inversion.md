# Premortem & Inversion

**One line:** Assume the plan already failed and work backward to why; or invert the goal and solve the opposite.

**Invented by:** Premortem — Gary Klein (2007), via prospective hindsight research (Mitchell, Russo & Pennington, 1989). Inversion — Carl Jacobi's *"invert, always invert"*, popularized by Charlie Munger.

## When to use

- Phase = SELECTING: choosing among options, de-risking a plan, stress-testing a favorite.
- You suspect you're too in love with an idea to see its failure modes.

## Part A — Premortem

1. **Jump to the future.** "It's a year from now. The project failed — completely, embarrassingly."
2. **Write the postmortem.** List the specific reasons it failed. Force at least 5. Be concrete ("users never came back after day 2 because onboarding asked for too much").
3. **Rank by (likelihood × damage).**
4. **Fix the top two now** — redesign the plan so those causes can't happen, or add an early signal that catches them.

The trick: *prospective hindsight* makes people name 30% more, and more specific, risks than "what could go wrong?" does — because a thing that has "already happened" is easier to explain than a thing that merely might.

## Part B — Inversion

Instead of "how do I make this succeed?", ask **"how would I guarantee it fails?"** — then avoid those things.

- Goal: "a thriving community forum." Inverted: "how do I make it a ghost town?" → no reason to return daily, newcomers ignored, no one in charge of welcome. Avoiding each = your real design brief.
- Goal: "write a gripping chapter." Inverted: "how do I bore the reader?" → nothing at stake, no change, info-dumping. Each is a fix list.

## Worked example

Choosing between three product ideas. Run a 5-line premortem on each. The one whose failure causes are *fixable with your resources* wins — not the one that sounded best. Often the premortem demotes the favorite and promotes the "boring" option whose risks you can actually control.

## Output shape

For premortem: the failure story, the ranked causes, the two fixes. For inversion: the inverted goal and the fix-list it generates. End with a recommendation, not just analysis.
