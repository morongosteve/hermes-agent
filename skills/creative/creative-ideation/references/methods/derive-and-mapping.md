# Dérive & Mapping

**One line:** Wander a space with no goal, follow what pulls you, and map the subjective terrain — using disorientation to discover what you actually want or where the real territory lies.

**Invented by:** Guy Debord and the Situationist International (*théorie de la dérive*, 1956) — the "drift" through a city led by its psychogeography rather than by errands or efficiency. Kin: *psychogeography*, the flâneur.

## When to use

- SELF domain: life decisions, career, "what should I study/do", open-ended personal direction.
- You're in **unknown territory** and a goal-directed plan would just re-run your existing assumptions. You need to *discover* the terrain before setting a destination.

## The idea

Goal-directed movement only ever reaches goals you already имели in mind; it can't find what you didn't know to look for. The dérive drops the goal: you move through a space (literal or metaphorical) pulled by attraction and repulsion — what draws you in, what you avoid — and you treat *those pulls as data*. The map you make afterward isn't geographic; it's a map of your own responses, and it reveals wants and directions that planning hides.

## The method (adapted for a life/career drift)

1. **Choose the terrain.** It can be a literal city walk, or a field to wander: a library section, a conference's hallway track, a week of saying yes to unplanned things, a随 sampling of courses/projects/people.
2. **Drop the goal.** No "I'm researching X." Move by pull: follow what's interesting *right now*, abandon what deadens.
3. **Log the pulls as you go.** What drew you in? What repelled you? Where did time disappear? Where did you get bored? Note it without judging whether it's "useful."
4. **Map the subjective terrain.** Afterward, lay the pulls out as a map: clusters of energy, dead zones, surprising adjacencies. This is a map of *you* against the territory.
5. **Read the map for a direction** — the direction isn't "the most pulls", it's where the pulls *converge* with something real you could pursue.

## Worked example

A burned-out engineer unsure what's next. Instead of a career plan, a two-week dérive: attend any talk/meetup/open-source thread that pulls, no filtering for "career relevance". Log: energy spikes around *teaching others to debug*, deadness around *scaling infra* (their current job). Map shows a convergence: explaining systems to people, repeatedly, is where time vanished. Direction surfaced: developer education / docs / devrel — invisible on the old plan. Grounded first step: write and publish one teaching thread, measure whether the energy survives contact with reality.

## Output shape

The terrain chosen, the logged pulls (attractions/repulsions), the map's clusters, and the direction where energy converges with something actionable — plus a small real first step to test it.
