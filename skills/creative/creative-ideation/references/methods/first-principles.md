# First Principles

**One line:** Strip a problem to its physical or logical bedrock — what must be true — and rebuild without the inherited assumptions everyone carries.

**Invented by:** Aristotle named the *first principle* (the basic proposition that can't be deduced from another). Descartes' methodic doubt; in modern practice associated with Feynman and with Elon Musk's "reason from first principles, not by analogy."

## When to use

- ARTIFACT (or any) domain with **accumulated assumptions** — "that's just how it's done", conventions no one questions, a cost everyone treats as fixed.
- Reasoning by analogy keeps landing you at the same place as everyone else.

## The idea

Most thinking is reasoning *by analogy* — "it's like what others do, with a tweak." That's efficient but it inherits every assumption baked into the thing you're copying, including the wrong ones. First-principles thinking refuses the inheritance: break the problem down to what is *actually, necessarily* true (physics, math, definitions, hard constraints), then reconstruct upward. The leverage is in discovering that an "unchangeable" constraint was just a convention.

## The method

1. **State the problem and the current solution** plainly.
2. **List every assumption** the current solution rests on — especially the invisible ones ("it has to be a store", "batteries are expensive", "resumes list jobs").
3. **Interrogate each: is it a law or a habit?** Separate the physically/logically necessary from the merely customary. Ask "why?" until you hit bedrock.
4. **Rebuild from the bedrock only.** Given *only* what's necessarily true, what's the best solution? Ignore how it's "normally" done.
5. **Reintroduce real constraints** one at a time, checking each is real.

## Worked example (the canonical one)

Problem: batteries cost ~$600/kWh, "so" grid storage is uneconomical.
- Assumption questioned: is $600 a law or a market price?
- Bedrock: a battery is just its material constituents — cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, a separator. Price those on the commodity market: ~$80/kWh.
- Rebuild: the gap between $80 (materials) and $600 (market) is manufacturing and supply chain — *changeable*, not a law of nature. So the problem is a production problem, not an impossibility. That reframing is the whole move.

## Worked example (non-engineering)

"A résumé must list your past jobs." Bedrock: an employer needs *evidence you can do the work*. Jobs are one proxy; a portfolio, a work sample, or a paid trial are others — several better. The convention was a habit, not a requirement.

## Output shape

The assumptions list (sorted law vs. habit), the bedrock, and the rebuilt solution — explicitly noting which "fixed" constraint turned out to be movable.
