# Creative Discipline (The Spine & The Box)

**One line:** A sprawling project is rescued by a daily habit, a single organizing idea (the "spine"), and ruthless cutting of everything that doesn't serve it.

**Invented by:** Twyla Tharp (*The Creative Habit*, 2003). The "box" and "spine" are hers.

## When to use

- Phase = REFINING, non-text domains (a choreography, a product, a film, an album, a talk) that have *too much* and no center.
- The user has lots of material and it isn't cohering.

## Three tools

### 1. The Box
Before a project, start a literal (or digital) box. Everything related goes in: clippings, objects, notes, references, false starts. The box is the project's memory and its permission to begin — "I have a box" beats "I have a blank page". It's not precious; it's a holding pen for raw material.

### 2. The Spine
Every work needs one organizing idea you could state in a sentence — the thing the piece is *about* underneath its subject. Tharp's *Movin' Out* spine was not "Billy Joel songs" but a specific emotional arc. When you're lost, you return to the spine and ask of each element: *does this serve it?* The spine is private; the audience never hears it, but they feel its absence.

### 3. Ruthless cutting
Most refinement is subtraction. If an element is beautiful but doesn't serve the spine, it goes ("kill your darlings"). The box keeps the cut material safe, which makes cutting psychologically possible.

## The method

1. **Gather** everything into the box (don't organize yet).
2. **Name the spine** in one sentence — what is this *really* about? If you can't, that's the problem; generate candidate spines and pick the one the strongest material supports.
3. **Audit every element against the spine.** Keep, cut, or park-in-box.
4. **Install a daily ritual** (Tharp's famous one: get in the cab at 5:30am — the habit *is* the discipline, not the talent). The ritual removes the daily decision to start.

## Worked example

A founder's demo talk is 40 slides and wandering.
- Box: dump every slide, anecdote, metric into one doc.
- Candidate spines: "our tech is clever" / "this customer's day got better". Pick the second — the material that lands supports it.
- Audit: 28 slides don't serve "this customer's day got better". Cut to 9. Park the tech-cleverness slides in the box for the engineering deep-dive, not this talk.

## Output shape

State the spine you'd choose and *why*, then the keep/cut verdict on the major elements, then a suggested daily ritual to sustain the work.
