# Biomimicry

**One line:** Ask how a living system already solved the function you need, and borrow its strategy.

**Invented by:** Named and systematized by Janine Benyus (*Biomimicry: Innovation Inspired by Nature*, 1997); long lineage (Velcro from burrs, 1941). The Biomimicry Institute's "Biologize the question" method.

## When to use

- ARTIFACT domain where the problem has a **natural-system analog** — anything about structure, materials, sensing, flow, self-repair, energy, cooperation.
- You want strategies honed by evolution over billions of years and typically running on ambient energy, benign materials, and local resources.

## The core discipline: function, not form

Amateur biomimicry copies *shapes* ("make it look like a shark"). Real biomimicry abstracts the **function** and asks how organisms *perform that function*, then translates the strategy. The question becomes: *"How does nature [verb]?"*

## The method

1. **Biologize the question.** Restate your design problem as a function in nature's terms. "How do we keep the building cool?" → *"How does nature regulate temperature without active cooling?"*
2. **Find biological strategies** that perform that function. (Termite mounds' passive ventilation; camel nostrils; leaf stomata; emperor penguins' huddle rotation.)
3. **Abstract the design principle** — strip the biology to the mechanism. Termite mounds: *a network of channels driven by internal heat gradients moves air with no fans.*
4. **Translate and emulate** in your material/context. Honor the deeper pattern too (nature runs on sunlight, uses few elements, recycles everything) — ask if your solution could as well.
5. **Evaluate against life's principles** — is it locally attuned, low-energy, benign? That check often improves the idea.

## Worked example

Problem: a water bottle that collects drinking water in arid air.
- Biologize: *"How does nature harvest water from fog/dew?"*
- Strategy: the Namib desert beetle's shell — hydrophilic bumps condense fog droplets, hydrophobic channels funnel them to its mouth.
- Abstract principle: *alternating water-attracting and water-repelling micro-surfaces condense and direct atmospheric moisture, passively.*
- Translate: a bottle (or tent panel) with a bumpy hydrophilic/hydrophobic coating that collects dew overnight — no power.
- Grounded first step: test the coating pattern on a cheap panel and measure mL/night before designing the vessel.

## Output shape

The biologized question, the organism(s) and their strategy, the abstracted principle, and the translation — plus a buildable first experiment.
