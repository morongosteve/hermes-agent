# OuLiPo (Constrained Writing)

**One line:** Formal constraints — self-imposed rules on letters, words, or structure — act as the engine that generates text.

**Invented by:** The Oulipo (*Ouvroir de littérature potentielle*), founded 1960 by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais. Members: Georges Perec, Italo Calvino, Harry Mathews, Jacques Roubaud.

## When to use

- TEXT domain, formal/poetry, or any time you want language to go somewhere your defaults won't take it.
- Naming things (titles, taglines) — constraints break the obvious phrasing.
- Paired with other methods to force freshness (see `defamiliarization`).

## The idea

A constraint is not a limit on creativity; it *is* the creativity. Forbidding the easy choice forces the interesting one. Perec wrote an entire novel (*La Disparition*) without the letter E — and the absence became the book's secret subject (loss, the missing). The rule does the inventing.

## A toolkit of constraints

- **Lipogram** — write without a chosen letter (hardest/best: E).
- **N+7** — replace every noun with the 7th noun after it in a dictionary. ("The cat sat on the mat" → "The cauldron sat on the matrix.") Instant estrangement.
- **Snowball** — each word one letter longer than the last: *I am the best, agony…*
- **Univocalic** — only one vowel allowed in the whole piece.
- **Palindrome** — reads the same backward.
- **Prisoner's constraint** — no letters with ascenders/descenders (as if saving paper).
- **Beautiful outlaw / belle absente** — each line omits one letter, spelling a hidden word across the poem.
- **Mandated vocabulary** — every line must contain a given word, or words drawn from a fixed set.

## The method

1. **Pick one constraint** that fits the subject (let the constraint comment on the theme if you can — E-less for a poem about absence).
2. **Write *into* the constraint**, letting it push word choices you'd never make freely.
3. **Don't cheat.** The friction is the value; a broken rule collapses the effect.
4. **Harvest the accidents.** The phrases the constraint forced are the gift — keep the best even if you drop the form.

## Worked example

Brief: a tagline for a late-night diner.
- Constraint: univocalic in **O** (only the vowel O).
- Working only with words whose single vowel is O, you grope past the obvious ("good", "food", "hot", "glow", "clocks") and the constraint hands you a line a free write never would: **"Hot food, low glow, no clocks."** That "no clocks" — the exact timeless, late-night-diner feeling — is the gift of the rule, not of inspiration.

## Output shape

Produce the constrained text itself, name the constraint, and flag the specific phrases the constraint generated that are worth keeping.

## Pairs well with

`chance-and-remix` (another formal/aleatory family), `defamiliarization` (constraint forces the strange word).
