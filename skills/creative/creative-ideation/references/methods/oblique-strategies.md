# Oblique Strategies

**One line:** Draw a single aphoristic card; it forces one lateral move on whatever you're stuck on.

**Invented by:** Brian Eno & Peter Schmidt (*Oblique Strategies: Over One Hundred Worthwhile Dilemmas*, first deck 1975).

## When to use

- Phase = UNBLOCKING: stuck mid-project, "going in circles", a decision paralyzed by too many options.
- OBJECT domain (music, visual art, performance) — the deck's native habitat.
- You need *one* decisive nudge, not a list of options.

## How it works

Each card is a short, often oblique instruction. You draw one at random, accept it, and apply it to your current stuck point — even (especially) if it seems irrelevant. The randomness defeats the loop your mind is stuck in; the obligation to *use* it forces a concrete move. You play **one card**, make the move, and continue. You don't shop for a card you like.

## A working subset of cards

Draw one (pick by gut or pseudo-randomly):

- *Honor thy error as a hidden intention.*
- *What would your closest friend do?*
- *Use an old idea.*
- *Work at a different speed.*
- *Emphasize the flaws.*
- *Remove specifics and convert to ambiguities.*
- *Do the words need changing?*
- *Repetition is a form of change.*
- *Look at the order in which you do things.*
- *Abandon normal instruments.*
- *What wouldn't you do?*
- *Make it more like itself / less like itself.*
- *Take away the elements in order of apparent non-importance.*
- *The most important thing is the thing most easily forgotten.*

## The method

1. Name the stuck point in one sentence.
2. Draw one card.
3. Interpret it *for this specific situation* — the translation is the creative act.
4. State the concrete next move it implies. One move.
5. If still stuck after acting, draw one more — never more than two in a row.

## Worked example

Stuck: "the second verse of the song is flat."
- Card drawn: *Repetition is a form of change.*
- Interpretation: don't write a new verse — repeat the first verse verbatim but change *one* thing around it (drop the drums, or pitch the vocal up an octave). Sameness reframes the words.
- Move: re-track verse 1's lyric over a stripped arrangement; decide after hearing it.

## Output shape

Name the card, give the situation-specific interpretation, give the single next move. Resist turning it into a list.
